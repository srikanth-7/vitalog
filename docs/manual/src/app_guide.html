1. What Vitalog is

Vitalog is a private family health record for lab reports. You pick a family member, add a lab report (camera photo, photo from the library, PDF, or almost any file), and the app reads it on the phone, pulls out every test (name, value, unit, reference range, high/low), files each test into a health group (Blood & CBC, Glucose, Kidney, Liver, Lipid, Thyroid, Vitamins, Iron, Urine and more), and shows it on a per-person timeline and dashboard. Over time it shows how each value changes.

It is not a medical provider: it never diagnoses, never invents a "health score", and only summarises what the lab printed.

The one-sentence promise: reports are read on this phone and nothing leaves it unless you tap Sync — and even then the server only stores sealed (end-to-end encrypted) boxes it cannot read.

Who it is for

  • A family member (often the adult child) who looks after the reports of parents, partner and children.
  • Older people themselves — hence Simple mode, plain-language permission prompts, big buttons, and Telugu / Hindi.
  • India-first: Indian lab formats (Thyrocare, SRL, Dr Lal PathLabs, Apollo, Metropolis), Indian number grouping (2,50,000), day-first dates, Indian reference sources.

The bottom tray (thumb zone)

"Read at the top, act at the bottom." One floating container holds the dock row — member switcher (always shows who you're viewing), Sync chip (on Reports), Add a report — and the tabs. The selected tab opens into a pill with its label over a sliding brand-coloured tile; the others show only their painted glyph. Each glyph plays a small moment when selected (the house draws a pulse, a tile becomes the dot, report lines scan in, the child hops). The dock hides on the Family tab.

Item actions

Long-press any item → bottom sheet of actions; a ⋯ button offers the same for discoverability. Menus are iOS-style pull-downs (showPullDownMenu); anything irreversible goes through an action-sheet (showDestructiveSheet) that says exactly what will be removed.

The 10-second rule

Shown the Home screen for 10 seconds, a stranger must be able to say "it's for family lab results". So the headline says what you're looking at ("Here's Maya's health snapshot"), the time greeting is the small line above, and empty screens show the health groups instead of a blank page.

Safety nets

  • Size caps: huge files and zip bombs are refused (including zip entries that lie about their size).
  • Timeout: reading stops after 90 seconds (timed out, Try again).
  • Vanished report: if the user deletes/cancels while reading, nothing is saved.
  • Leftover copies: temporary scan-*.pdf files left by a killed upload are swept on next start; Android cache copies are deleted after reading.
  • Duplicates: the same file is caught on upload (file hash); the same paper report from another file is caught after reading (same member, printed date, lab, and every test with the same value). The copy is dropped and "View" opens the one kept. Undated reports are never called duplicates.
  • Wrong person: the parser reads the printed patient name (kept on the phone only). If it doesn't match the member's first name, the timeline offers Keep here / Move to….

6. The parser and categories

LabReportParser is rule-based (no AI). Input: one string per printed row, columns joined by two spaces.

  • A row is a test if its start matches a catalog alias (~300 spellings in lab_test_catalog.dart, matched in one pass with an AliasTrie; longest wins).
  • Unknown tests are accepted only under a recognised section heading and with value + unit + range. A complete row the catalog doesn't know and that isn't under a known heading goes to Other as Needs review; the user files it once and the choice is remembered (encrypted) for later reports.
  • Values must be standalone numbers (so "25-OH" or "T3" aren't values). Indian grouping 2,50,000 is supported.
  • Status: printed H/L flag wins; otherwise value vs. printed range. Ranges printed per sex ("M: 13–17 F: 12–15.5") are judged with the member's sex.
  • Dates are day-first (India/UK) unless impossible; date-of-birth lines are ignored. Report date, collection date and upload date are kept separate — upload date is never used as a medical date.
  • Lab name is cut at the first address/contact marker; unknown test names lose phones, emails and links (Redactor).
  • OCR quirks handled: Cyrillic-misread units → catalog's typical unit; HIU/mL → µIU/mL.

Categories (predefined, 17 groups)

Defined in domain/health_categories.dart, each with its key tests. The Categories tab shows Tested groups (with "Not yet: …" listing missing key tests) and then greyed Yet to test groups. "On advice" groups — pancreas, cancer markers, hormones, infections, clotting — list example tests under "When your doctor advises" and are never flagged as missing. Key tests only point out what's missing; they never judge results. A test can belong to more than one group.

7. Judging results: printed and usual ranges

  1. The printed range always wins (and a printed H/L flag wins over everything).
  2. If no range is printed, the repository tries a built-in usual range (domain/reference/, compiled as constants; sources: WHO, Indian, ADA/RSSDI, ATP III, KDIGO; the only source document is docs/REFERENCE_RANGES.md).
  3. A usual range is applied only when unit, sex and age band are all known — it never guesses. Adult ranges are skipped for under-18s (except the WHO haemoglobin cut-off).
  4. Results judged this way show usual range wording and the source — never "per report". This judgement stays on the phone and is never synced.

Future range updates from a server must pass verifyRangeTable: an Ed25519 signature check with the public key shipped in the app, and no downgrade to older editions. The private signing seed lives in the original Mac's Keychain.

Open: the built-in ranges need a clinician's sign-off before launch; pediatric ranges need clinical sources.

8. Home dashboard, charts and trends

Home order: greeting → At a glance / Needs attention → trends → latest report → categories → recent results.

  • At a glance: hero count of latest results within range, a stacked status bar, and a legend with icon + word + count (never colour-only).
  • Needs attention: latest high/low results. No confetti when anything needs attention.
  • Trend tiles: latest value, change since previous, a sparkline drawn over the printed-range band; tap → the test's history. After 3 moves the same way a tile says "Up 3 times in a row" / "Down…" — it states what happened, never what it means. Readings in different units are never mixed.
  • Category tiles: count each test's latest high/low as "N outside range".
  • Test history: every dated value, a chart of same-unit readings over the range band, Edit at the bottom.
  • Chart colours are dedicated chart* tokens validated for colour-blind distinguishability in both themes.
  • Pure helpers (latestPerTest, StatusTally, trendsFor, trendStreak, parseRange) are unit-tested.
  • Trends are hidden in Simple mode and on a single report's dashboard. Home sections can be hidden in settings; hidden sections aren't built at all.

Tapping a report on the timeline opens the report dashboard: the same Home screen narrowed to that report's results (always shows every section, updates live while reading).

9. Editing, review and "wrong person"

  • Edit (test detail → bottom bar): fix value, unit, range or category. updateResult re-judges the status, marks the result edited, and flips a Needs review report to Ready once nothing is left to review.
  • Looks right: confirms a flagged result without changing it.
  • Compare with original: shows the original document next to the value — decrypted in memory only.
  • Category picker: files an unknown test; remembered by lowercased test name for future reports.
  • Move to…: moves a report to another member (assignReport), re-judging usual ranges for the new person's sex/age.

10. Family members, photos, duplicates

  • Add (Family tab or the member switcher): name, relation (stored as a fixed key so it translates), age (stored as a birth year so it stays current), sex (or Not set); photo via Edit details. Saved encrypted in members.json.
  • Edit details: long-press on the Family tab; re-judges usual ranges.
  • Remove: if the member has reports, one confirmation names how many go; removes reports, results, originals, photo and (when signed in) their server rows. For a shared member, removing means leaving — shared reports stay for the others.
  • Photos: photos/<id>.bin, encrypted, never in members.json or anything synced.
  • Switching: from the dock keeps the current tab; from the Family list goes to Home.
  • Real installs start empty (no sample family). The sample family (Maya, Arjun, Anika, Meera) exists only in tests and the manual's screenshots.

11. Reminders

lib/features/reminders/: phone-only scheduled notifications (e.g. "Next thyroid test"). Stored encrypted in reminders.json. RemindersCubit schedules the next 4 occurrences per reminder and re-syncs on start and whenever members change (a removed member's reminders go). Entry points: the bell on Home (with a dot when one is due within 7 days) and the Family tab.

Privacy on the lock screen: only the user's own title and "Reminder for {name}" — never test names or values. Before Android's permission prompt the app explains why; after a refusal it offers Open Settings.

Not built: reminders that reach other family members' phones (needs sharing to be live).

14. Accounts, Sync v2 and the recovery key

Sign-in

Optional. Email/password (confirmation email, reset) or native Google sign-in (google_sign_in → signInWithIdToken). Builds without the Supabase --dart-defines run fully offline and say "Cloud sync isn't set up in this build yet."

Sync v2 (sealed)

  1. Every family profile has its own random AES-256 profile key.
  2. Each report (with its results) and each member is sealed with that key on the phone.
  3. Profile keys are wrapped for the account's X25519 public key.
  4. Server tables: account_keys, profile_keys, sealed_profiles, sealed_reports. The server sees opaque ids, sizes and timestamps — never names, lab, file names, test names, values, ranges or dates.
  5. Data stays on the phone after Sync. Originals never upload.
  6. Deleting a report or member deletes its server rows (real deletes). Delete account (Family tab) removes everything via the delete-account edge function.

The account key and recovery key

The account's private key lives in the Keychain (iOS: synchronizable → iCloud Keychain, so a new iPhone gets it) plus a recovery key shown once after the first Sync ("Save to my passwords" or Copy; again from the Family tab). A phone without the key asks "Enter your recovery key". The account key is never replaced — that would orphan every sealed row.

Restore

No button: signing in quietly pulls and merges the account's reports, results and members. Nothing is uploaded until the user taps Sync.

Status: built and unit-tested, but the Supabase projects (staging, prod) haven't had schema.sql + hardening.sql applied yet, so Sync, restore and invites have not been tried end to end.

15. Family sharing and invites

  • Equal access: no owner. Anyone with access can add reports, remove someone, or leave.
  • Invite: long-press a member → Share → family card → Invite someone. The app syncs that member, then opens the share sheet with https://<invite host>/i/<id>#<secret>. The secret is in the #fragment, which browsers never send to a server.
  • Claim: the link opens the app (app_links), or the user pastes it via Family → Open an invite link. The claim screen shows a preview (sealed with the link's secret), asks "Who is X to you?", and on accept pulls the profile and its reports and opens Home on them. Single use, expiry, rate limit enforced by the server (open_invite).
  • Combine: accepting as "You" when the phone already has a "You" offers Combine / Keep separate; combine moves reports across, drops a file already present, keeps your own name/sex/birth year/photo/reminders.
  • Family card: who has access (with the name each person goes by), pending invites with Cancel, Remove.
Launch blockers: buy a new invite domain (vitalog.app is taken — docs/INVITE_DOMAIN.md), host invite_site/, verified App Links + iOS Associated Domains (paid Apple team), then remove the com.vitalog.app:// hand-over button. Not built: key rotation after removal, notifications to others on remove/combine.

16. Big screens: iPhone Duo and foldables

  • Side layout when width ≥ 700 and wider than tall. Upright and folded keep the phone layout.
  • Side tray: the bottom tray stood on its side on the left edge (product owner's call over Apple's right-side default): person on top, vertical tabs, Sync on Reports, Add report at the bottom. Content keeps clear of the Duo's right-hand status column.
  • Two panes split at the fold: Home = status | history; Categories = groups | results; Reports = timeline | report; Family = people | settings. Navigation opens into the right pane.
  • Tablets (> 736 pt) use a centred 640 pt reading column.

17. Design system and motion

  • Logo = splash = launcher icon: a pulse trace forming a "V" over ruled log lines, ending in a coral "latest reading" dot. The splash slides the lines in, draws the trace, pops the dot and beats it (~67 bpm) as the loader, then lifts away. Brand colour #173D35.
  • Shapes: Apple-style continuous corners (AppRadius 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 26 / 30, superellipse). Nested shapes are concentric (inner = outer − gap).
  • Colours: AppColors tokens for light and dark, accents, a coral reading token, dedicated chart tokens.
  • Motion primitives: Reveal (staggered entry), CountUpText, ScanBeam (report being read), celebrate() (confetti + haptic when a report is ready and nothing needs attention), PressScale/TapCard (press feedback with a haptic tick). All honour Reduce Motion.
  • Empty states: illustrations that assemble and idle like the onboarding pictures (~20 s, then rest), centred, with the health groups shown so the app explains itself.
  • Onboarding: 3 welcome pages whose illustrations assemble with the swipe, then the sign-in offer ("Not now" skips it), then a spotlight tour (replay: "Take a quick tour").
